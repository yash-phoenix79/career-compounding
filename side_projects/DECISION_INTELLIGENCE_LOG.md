# Decision Intelligence App — Personal Log

**Status:** Idea captured; no implementation or tool choice yet.

## Why I want this

I want to build or adopt a personal decision-intelligence app that I can consult when planning or making important real-life decisions. ChatGPT can already answer questions, but I often worry that I am overlooking obvious considerations, hidden assumptions, second-order effects, or options I did not think to ask about.

The goal is not simply to get an answer. It is to discover what I may be missing, including the **unknown unknowns**.

## What I want it to do

When I provide a situation and decision question, it should help me:
- Ask better questions and clarify what actually matters.
- Identify missing information, assumptions, blind spots, and overlooked alternatives.
- Research important claims and distinguish evidence from speculation.
- Challenge the leading recommendation and surface failure modes.
- Think through consequences, downside, and second-order effects.
- Give me a reasoned recommendation and practical next steps.
- Record decisions and later compare expectations with outcomes.

## Thinking lens

Use Nassim Nicholas Taleb's ideas where relevant, especially **Black Swan thinking**: consider rare, high-impact events; avoid pretending the future is fully predictable; examine fragility, exposure to downside, and what could go badly in ways I have not anticipated.

The app should not claim it can predict every Black Swan. It should help expose uncertainty, stress-test plans, and make decisions more robust when predictions fail.

## Constraints for now

- Keep this simple. Do not turn the idea into a large architecture project prematurely.
- Building from scratch and adopting an existing open-source tool are both valid options.
- Prefer practical, evidence-backed usefulness over agent count or technical sophistication.
- No technology choices or implementation commitment have been made yet.

## Next step

Keep this as the starting log. Before building, clarify only the few constraints that materially affect whether to adopt or build. Then test whether the workflow catches important omissions better than an ordinary ChatGPT conversation.
